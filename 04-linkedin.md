# A defensible test is one a reviewer can rebuild without you in the room

*Platform: LinkedIn · Audience: teams and training buyers · Source: Publishing_Kit.md v1.3*

---

I've learned the hard way that a defensible test isn't just one that finds issues — it's one a reviewer can rebuild without me in the room.

So I wrote down the three things most writeups skip: decide the scope in writing before you touch anything, stop at proof, and keep a record that separates what you found from what you guessed and what you ruled out.

It's built only on public frameworks (NIST SP 800-115, OWASP, MITRE CWE/ATT&CK, FIRST CVSS), and it's for defensive, educational use — no real targets, no working payloads.

Free to read: https://friendly-skiff-f57.notion.site/
